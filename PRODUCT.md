## Platform

web

## Users

- **People who are not data analysts**: a buyer, a journalist, someone curious. They arrive with one
  question, such as how many of a model were registered last month or whether electric vehicles are
  growing, often on a phone, and want one clear answer and the evidence for it.
- **The motor trade and informed buyers**: dealers, importers, brand staff. When a month's figures land
  they check totals, brand and model rankings, new against used imports, the change on a year ago, and
  their own brand against its rivals. Today they download the register, clean it and redraw it by hand.
- **Analysts**: they want to build their own charts from any field the data can honestly answer, keep
  them, and share them.

## Product Purpose

The place people go for a reliable answer about New Zealand's vehicle market, built on the NZ Transport
Agency's open registration data. A visitor asks one question and gets one clear answer, the evidence for
it, and the freedom to dig further. Success: a visitor can answer a question the site's makers did not
plan for, without touching a multi-gigabyte file, and trusts the figure.

## Positioning

- The visitor decides what to look at. Any measure, any field, any filter and any period the data can
  honestly answer can be asked; nothing fixes the visitor to views the makers chose (the owner, 2026-11-18
  and 2026-11-27).
- Every build sets its figures beside those other organisations publish and shows the result (388 of 389
  within tolerance for October 2026).
- Every sentence about the data is generated by rule from the figures. It says what the data shows,
  never why, and never whether it is good.
- No vehicle row reaches the browser; no tracking, cookies or third-party requests.

## Operating Context

- NZTA refreshes the register monthly; the pipeline builds each month once and the site redraws on the
  newest month. A saved question keeps itself up to date, or stays pinned to a snapshot when a figure is
  being quoted.
- The trade reads new vehicles and used imports apart. A combined figure shown without the split reads as
  wrong (the owner, 2026-11-25).
- The competitor the owner measures against is the Motor Trade Association's vstat site.
- Static site (Astro, React islands, Observable Plot) on Cloudflare Pages. Nothing is deployed yet; the
  address is vcore.co.nz.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Six chart shapes and no seventh: mix, trend, league, distribution, geo, matrix. One rule table in config
  says which fit a question; a shape that does not fit is shown greyed with a one-line reason, never drawn.
- A canvas page holds several charts the visitor adds, arranges and removes. One question per chart.
- Every figure is one step from its definition, its source and its snapshot date. A caveat is one line
  with a link.
- New vehicles and used imports are shown apart, defaulting to new; brand against brand is offered.
- A flow series never shares a chart with survivors. A measurement artefact is never presented as market
  behaviour; dated events are marked beside the data, with no interpretation.
- The first chart draws within 3 seconds on a throttled phone profile, checked on every change.
- Existing canvas and Build links keep opening the same charts.
- Terms: "registrations", never "sales"; "new" and "used imports"; "powertrain"; "brand" for make.
- Undecided: whether work order 3's date (public-ready by 12 December 2026) still governs going public
  (inferred: no).

## Evidence on Hand

- The published cube under `public/data/` (gitignored locally; releases `cube-<month>` on GitHub): monthly
  counts by brand, model, powertrain, buyer type, origin and more, from January 2021 for new registrations.
- `reconciliation.json` in each build: the published figures set beside ours.
- `data/reference/events.csv`: 54 dated policy, market and maker events.
- `data/reference/display_names.csv`: how makes and powertrains are written for a reader.
- No testimonials, customers, press or usage figures exist. None may be invented.

## Product Principles

1. The visitor decides. Defaults do the work, and every default can be changed where it stands.
2. The answer first: a sentence and a figure, then the evidence.
3. Say what the data shows, never why.
4. What cannot be asked says why, and how to ask something that can.
5. Trust is the product: a wrong figure costs more than a late one.
